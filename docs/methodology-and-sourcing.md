# How our plant data is made

*A plain-language look at where plant.lifestyle's growing information comes from and how we check it.*

We think you deserve to know why a planting date, a spacing number, or a watering tip says what it
says. This page explains how every crop in plant.lifestyle is researched, written, and verified, and
where we draw the line on honesty.

## Where the information comes from

Our certified crops are grounded in **Tier-1 university cooperative extension sources**: the
research-based, publicly funded gardening guidance published by land-grant universities and their
extension services (for example, Clemson, Cornell, the University of Minnesota, Utah State, Texas
A&M, and many more). These are the same trusted references that master gardeners and extension
agents use.

Where a claim rests on widely accepted horticultural knowledge rather than one specific page, or on
long-standing gardening tradition, we say so plainly rather than dressing it up as a hard citation.
Seed-company catalogs and almanacs can be helpful, but we treat them as background, not as the
authority behind a certified fact.

## How each crop is built and checked

Every crop moves through the same pipeline before it can go live:

1. **Authored from a proven model.** A new crop is written by starting from a closely related crop we
   have already certified (a nectarine begins from our peach model, a cantaloupe from our watermelon
   model), then re-researching every value for the new crop. The related crop is scaffolding; the
   biology is redone from the ground up.
2. **Checked by an automated gate suite.** Before a human ever reviews it, each crop passes through
   dozens of automated checks that catch structural problems, missing fields, contradictory numbers,
   and, importantly, planting calendars that are not logically possible. These checks prove the data
   is well-formed and internally consistent. They do not invent facts; that is what the human review
   is for.
3. **Reviewed for biological accuracy.** A person reads the crop against its sources, looking for the
   subtle failure we care most about: a number that is attributed to a source which does not actually
   say it. Getting the biology honest, not just plausible, is the bar.
4. **Source-checked, then certified.** We re-open a sample of the cited sources and confirm the
   numbers hold. Only then is a crop marked certified and allowed to appear on the site.

A crop that has not cleared this whole process is kept as a draft and is not shown to you.

## The planting calendar

The planting calendar is the heart of plant.lifestyle: your reminders and timing all run off it, so
we hold it to a high standard. Dates are specific to your region and climate zone, not a single
national average. We also run a dedicated check that a calendar makes physical sense over the course
of a year, for example that a crop is never shown "growing" in a month when nothing was ever planted,
and that a continuous harvest window is not missing a month in the middle. When our own review turned
up calendar quirks like these, we fixed them at the source and added a permanent guard so they cannot
come back.

## What we are honest about

Good gardening information is honest about its own limits. A few things we flag rather than hide:

- **Modeled regional windows.** Extension services do not publish a planting chart for every crop in
  every region. Where a specific chart does not exist, we build the window from the crop's frost
  tolerance, its days to maturity, and the nearest published guidance, and we note that it is modeled.
- **Marginal regions.** Some crops are a stretch in a given climate (a chill-loving cherry in a warm
  southern zone, a heat-loving melon in a cool northern one). We give the best-effort window and say
  plainly that the region is marginal, rather than promising a harvest we cannot.
- **Traditional wisdom.** Gardening has a deep well of grower experience that no study has formally
  measured. When we pass along that kind of tradition, we frame it as tradition ("many growers
  find..."), not as established fact.
- **Variety-level detail.** Fine differences between named varieties are refined over time. Early on,
  a variety figure may reflect common catalog norms rather than a variety-specific study, and we say so.

## Certified versus in progress

We are expanding the library steadily. A **certified** crop has cleared the full pipeline above and
is safe to plan around. Crops still in progress are held back until they are certified. As the
library grows, the certified count grows with it, and every certified crop carries its own record of
the sources and judgment calls behind it.

## Questions or corrections

If something looks off for your area, tell us. Local knowledge is exactly the kind of signal that
makes this better, and a careful correction from a real grower is worth more than any automated check.
