## 1. The precompute worry inverts

PLA-122 flagged that the melon cells are `resolution_method: static_precompute` and warned that
patching the resolved string leaves the upstream rule (`plant_out` from `last_frost`,
`offset_days: 14`) able to regenerate Feb 1. **Measured before touching anything, and it is the
other way round:**

| cell | declared `last_frost` | rule gives | stored before | stored after |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| cantaloupe, watermelon z9 + z10 | Jan 31 | **Feb 14** | Feb 1 | **Feb 15** |

**The rule never produced Feb 1.** Jan 31 + 14 = Feb 14, so the *stored* value was the one that did
not reproduce, 13 days out from its own declared anchor. This ruling moves the cells to within one
day of what their own arithmetic already implies.

So AZ1005's first seed mark (Feb 15) and the cell's frost arithmetic (Feb 14) are **two independent
lines landing in the same place**, and the edit makes these cells *more* reproducible, not less. No
offset needs reconciling. A guard (G1) pins the relationship at ±1 day so a later offset change
cannot silently break it — and that guard could not have been written before this edit, because the
pre-state failed it by 13 days.

**Nothing re-runs the precompute over existing cells**, checked directly: `build_region_shells.py`
reshapes key-sets idempotently and no-clobber; `build_mid_south_cells.py` is a one-off region build;
every other tool touching `offset_days` alongside `resolved_by_zone` is a one-off promote, a gate or
a scan. `frost_anchor_reproduction_gate` reports 0 violations over 1,409 cells and cannot be tripped
by this edit, since it fires only when two or more arm types agree on a *different* anchor.

Both zones declare `last_frost: Jan 31` — note they do **not** use `zone_frost_data`'s zone-10
Jan 15. The region carries a single anchor across both zones, which is why both cells held the same
string despite different USDA zones.

## 2. Turnip was a display reconciliation, not a data change

This is the check PLA-122 asked for, and the answer is unambiguous:

| field | before | source says | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| `first_plant_date` | `Sep 1` | Sep | already correct |
| `last_plant_date` | `May 31` | May | **already correct** |
| `plant_out` (display) | `Sep - Oct` | Sep–May | **stale** |

The sourced window was in the data all along; only the human-readable string understated it. It now
reads `Sep - May`, the wrap-around format **this crop's own `harvest` string already uses**
(`Nov - May`). A guard (G4) asserts `first_plant_date` and `last_plant_date` do *not* move, which is
what makes "display reconciliation" a checkable claim rather than a description.

**Checked and deliberately not changed:** the `calendar` array still marks Sep and Oct `plant` and
Nov through May `harvest`. Those months are now both, and a calendar cell carries **one token per
month**, so the overlap cannot be expressed. Harvest is the more useful signal for a reader standing
in January. That is the ribbon-granularity limit PLA-122 already identified as an app-rendering
question in the Code lane, and it is recorded rather than papered over.

## 3. The other three rulings

**Watermelon's summer planting is declared, not dropped.** AZ1005 marks low-desert watermelon at
Feb 15, Mar 1 and Mar 15 and nothing else all year — a document *actively indicating* no second
window rather than merely being silent about one, which is stronger than absence. Dropping it
outright was offered and not taken. The finding flips to `accepted_modeled`; the two
`second_planting` nodes stay on the institution root, because AZ1005 contradicts them.

**Garlic's harvest stays at Apr 13, declared modeled.** No new source exists — both the RGV
Homeowner Vegetable Guide 2022 and *Vegetable Crops of the Lower Rio Grande Valley* were read
directly and neither lists garlic at all, consistent with campaign C's zero-rows finding. Keeping
Apr 13 with an explicit label beats reverting to a figure known to be derived for a colder region
(Bexar County and our own se_gulf rows say May–June; the Valley is genuinely warmer than both).
`rgv` gains a `plantings_provenance` block in the `{model, basis, supersedes}` shape its own other
thirteen regions already use.

**Lavender is declared modeled.** No university-published source gives a planting window for
*Lavandula* in the desert Southwest; what exists is consumer/blog tier and not T1-admissible. Those
non-citable sources converge on late fall or early spring avoiding May–September, which brackets the
existing window, so it is probably not wrong — only uncited. More searching will not find this.

**Not in this patch:** the same lavender finding records two mislabelled source ids (`nmsu_chart`
pointing at `lowwaterplants.nmsu.edu`, `nmsu_donaana_mg` pointing at RR-770). Fixing them needs new
catalog ids minted — structural lane, own issue.

## 4. Verification

`whole_crop_gate` PASS ×5, `gate_all` **121/121**, `release_verify` "no new violations" with
`catalog +none -none` and its user-facing dash/spelled-degrees scan clean (which matters here, since

3. **G3** (melon harvest arms and `second_planting` untouched) could not fail for the same reason;

4. **G7's footprint check** needed `CROPS` shrunk to make the real footprint disagree with the

That is **six instances of the computed-expected-value shape across three promotes in one day**,
every one caught only by neutering the guard and watching the suite stay green. The rule stands and
is now demonstrated rather than asserted: *when a guard's expected value is computed rather than
written down, assume it is vacuous until a mutation proves otherwise.*

## 5. Left open

**The `uscrn_validation` cross-check PLA-122 wanted for items 1 and 2 does not exist** — the
conclusion is right, and the diagnosis in the issue is not. Measured roster-wide:

- cantaloupe and watermelon carry **zero** `uscrn_validation` records, not 22 filed under other
  regions.
- The desert regions are **not** the gap: `warm_arid` has 16 records, `low_desert_az` 8,
  `ca_desert` 8, across 220 records on 38 of 128 crops.
- **All 220 records sit on `direct_sow` arms**, which is PLA-110's deliberate scope. The three
  melons model **every one of their 17 regions as `plant_out`** and carry no `direct_sow` arm
  anywhere, so they are structurally outside USCRN's reach.

So the two melon rulings were indeed made without that tiebreaker, and no amount of desert-region
backfill would have supplied it.

**The interesting part is the modeling mismatch this exposes.** AZ1005 marks both melon rows **S,
its token for seeds**, and the Cameron County and LRGV guides likewise treat melons as sown. Our
data models them as transplants in all 17 regions. For a direct-sown cucurbit, soil temperature is
the governing anchor — the `desert-cucurbits-planted-on-last-frost` lesson — so these are exactly
the crops the USCRN table was built to validate, and the `plant_out` modeling is what puts them out
of reach. Worth its own issue: measure how many crops the sources treat as direct-sown while the
dataset models them as transplanted.

Also open: the two mislabelled lavender source ids (needs catalog ids minted), and the ribbon
granularity question for months that are both plant and harvest.
