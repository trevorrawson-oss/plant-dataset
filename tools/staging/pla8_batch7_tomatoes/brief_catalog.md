# CONTROL-METHOD CATALOG -- the ONLY 56 methods a rung may name.
# Ladder order is by tier: cultural < physical < biological < soft_chemical < conventional.

## cultural
  airflow_spacing              applies_to=['bacterial', 'disease_general', 'fungal_foliar']
      MEANS: Preventive layout for the diseases that need damp, still air, set at planting by spacing and site choice. Downy mildew and the celery leaf-spot blights are typical, and the same room helps against gray mold, powdery mildew and damping-off in a crowded tray.
  avoid_ammoniacal_nitrogen    applies_to=['physiological']
      MEANS: Sidedressing tomatoes, peppers and other fruiting crops where blossom end rot has appeared before, especially early in the season when fruit is sizing.
      CAUTION: Soil pH governs how much calcium is available in the first place, so a soil test is worth more than a fertilizer swap if the disorder recurs every year.
  balance_nitrogen             applies_to=['fungal_foliar', 'fungal_soilborne', 'insect_soft_bodied']
      MEANS: A preventive feeding habit anywhere aphids and other soft-bodied sap-suckers turn up most years, and on bushy or vining crops with a history of canopy rots such as white mold. Heavy nitrogen pushes out both the soft new growth aphids multiply on and the dense growth that keeps a canopy wet, whatever the crop.
  bottom_watering              applies_to=['fungal_soilborne', 'insect_general']
      MEANS: Indoor trays and seedlings, especially microgreens, where a wet surface drives damping-off and fungus gnats and there is no rescue spray for a raw crop.
  certified_clean_stock        applies_to=['bacterial', 'disease_general', 'fungal_foliar', 'fungal_soilborne', 'viral']
      MEANS: Diseases that travel in the planting material itself: seed-borne foliar and vascular pathogens, and viruses carried in cuttings, crowns or divisions. Set once, at purchase or propagation, before anything is in the ground.
  crop_rotation                applies_to=['any']
      MEANS: A season-to-season foundation wherever a problem survives in the bed between crops, whether it waits in the soil or in last year's debris. Clubroot, black rot and cabbage maggot are the classic cases, and the same move works on cucumber beetles and on the leaf diseases that carry over in old vines.
      CAUTION: Clubroot resting spores persist in soil for years, so a rotation of 5 to 7 years away from the cabbage family is needed to help
  even_watering                applies_to=['mite', 'physiological']
      MEANS: Steady soil moisture instead of a swing between soaked and bone dry. It prevents the calcium-movement disorders such as celery blackheart and blossom-end rot, at roughly 1 to 2 inches per week on shallow-rooted crops, and it holds spider mites down too, since they build up fastest on plants that have been left dry and stressed.
      CAUTION: Blackheart can strike even with adequate soil calcium if watering is uneven, so consistent moisture matters more than adding calcium
  garden_sanitation            applies_to=['any']
      MEANS: End-of-season cleanup, plus pulling the first affected leaves or fruit during the season. It is the backbone step for anything that spends the winter in the debris you leave behind, from black rot and other diseases with no chemical cure to beetles, borers and slugs sheltering in what is still standing.
      CAUTION: Destroy diseased debris rather than leaving or burying it; infected tissue can still carry the pathogen to the next crop
  improve_drainage             applies_to=['disease_general', 'fungal_soilborne']
      MEANS: Any bed with a history of plants wilting and not recovering after watering, and as standard practice on heavy or low-lying ground before planting anything susceptible.
  moisture_buffering_mulch     applies_to=['physiological']
      MEANS: Fruiting crops with a history of blossom end rot or of splitting, mulched at planting. Distinct from the straw mulch used under strawberries, which is a splash and contact barrier for fruit rots rather than a moisture buffer.
  off_season_tillage           applies_to=['insect_chewing', 'insect_general']
      MEANS: A finished bed that carried a pest which stays put over the winter, worked once after harvest. That may be pupae in the soil, as with tomato hornworm, or caterpillars inside old stalks, as with European corn borer, where shredding and turning the stalks under is what reaches them. Distinct from garden sanitation, which clears the surface rather than breaking up what sits in it.
      CAUTION: Tillage works against no-dig beds and against soil structure and earthworms, so weigh it where the pest pressure is light and handpicking is keeping up
  planting_time_avoidance      applies_to=['insect_boring', 'insect_chewing']
      MEANS: A pest whose damage falls in a predictable, locally published stretch of the season, on a crop quick enough to finish before it or start after it. The two documented cases differ in shape: the squash vine borer has a single flight to get behind, while the Mexican bean beetle runs several generations a year whose damage still concentrates in July and August. Distinct from crop rotation, which moves the planting in space; this one moves it in time.
      CAUTION: Emergence dates shift with the region and the season, so treat a published window as a starting point and confirm it locally rather than carrying a date across regions
  prompt_harvest               applies_to=['disease_general', 'fungal_foliar', 'insect_general', 'vertebrate']
      MEANS: Fruit crops where over-ripe or fallen fruit feeds the problem: fig souring, fruit rots, and sap beetles, and where birds and squirrels take ripe fruit. Distinct from garden sanitation, which removes debris and culls you never intended to eat; this is about taking the crop you do want, sooner.
  raise_soil_ph                applies_to=['fungal_soilborne']
      MEANS: For clubroot in the cabbage family, paired with long rotation and resistant varieties; there is no chemical cure, so this is a core cultural tool.
      CAUTION: Lime this high only to fight clubroot or to correct a tested deficiency; most vegetables prefer a pH near 6.0 to 6.8, so do not over-lime the whole garden
  reflective_mulch             applies_to=['insect_soft_bodied', 'viral']
      MEANS: Vegetables with a local history of aphid-transmitted virus, laid at planting while the crop is still small. Squash, melon and cucumber are the documented cases.
  resistant_rootstock          applies_to=['disease_general', 'fungal_soilborne']
      MEANS: A susceptible variety you want to keep growing on ground where a soilborne wilt is already established. Distinct from resistant varieties, which is choosing a different cultivar outright; here you keep the cultivar and change its roots.
  resistant_varieties          applies_to=['any']
      MEANS: Chosen at seed-buying time, and worth checking first for any problem that returns year after year. Most often disease resistance, such as clubroot, downy mildew and black rot, but it also covers varieties a pest is less drawn to, like a tight corn husk against earworm. The natural handoff to variety-level resistance data.
      CAUTION: Clubroot-resistant varieties tolerate rather than fully resist the disease
  sensible_seeding_rate        applies_to=['disease_general', 'fungal_soilborne']
      MEANS: Prevention for damping-off in trays, seedbeds, and dense crops such as microgreens, set at sowing by not overseeding.
  sound_sowing_practice        applies_to=['disease_general', 'fungal_soilborne']
      MEANS: Direct-sown beds and seed trays with a damping-off history, set at sowing by seed quality, depth, soil warmth and restrained watering. Distinct from sensible seeding rate, which is about sowing DENSITY and crowding rather than about how fast the seed comes up.
  straw_mulch                  applies_to=['disease_general', 'fungal_foliar']
      MEANS: A foundational cultural step for gray mold and fruit rots on strawberries, laid to keep the berries off the soil and reduce splash, alongside good spacing and airflow.
  water_at_the_base            applies_to=['bacterial', 'fungal_foliar', 'fungal_soilborne', 'mollusk']
      MEANS: Outdoor plantings with splash-dispersed foliar disease, and beds where slugs are sustained by a surface that stays damp. Distinct from bottom watering, which is a tray and seedling technique for keeping a potting mix surface dry.
  weed_host_control            applies_to=['fungal_foliar', 'insect_soft_bodied']
      MEANS: A bed with a history of aphids, thrips, or a foliar fungus whose weedy relatives grow nearby, cleared before sowing and kept clear at the edges. Distinct from garden sanitation, which removes the CROP's own debris, mostly after harvest; this removes OTHER plants that host the problem, mostly before the crop is up.
      CAUTION: The relationship is host-specific rather than general tidiness, so it is worth knowing which weeds host the problem before clearing ground that was doing no harm
  wet_foliage_discipline       applies_to=['bacterial', 'fungal_foliar']
      MEANS: Any bed carrying a bacterial or splash-spread foliar disease, applied by choosing WHEN you work rather than what you apply. Distinct from watering at the base, which changes where the water goes; this one changes where you go, and it still applies after rain or dew, which no irrigation choice controls.
      CAUTION: This lowers the rate of spread rather than stopping an infection that is already established, so it belongs alongside the other steps rather than in place of them
      CAUTION: Powdery mildew is the exception and this does not act on it: USU notes that powdery mildews do not spread in rain or free water, needing only high humidity or dew for a few hours, so on that one disease it is airflow and spacing that do the work here, not staying out of a wet planting.

## physical
  bird_netting                 applies_to=['vertebrate']
      MEANS: The reliable way to protect ripening strawberries from birds, on a hoop frame that holds the net off the fruit with the edges sealed to the ground.
  bird_scare_deterrents        applies_to=['vertebrate']
      MEANS: A cheap first line against birds on a strawberry patch, rotating shiny and noise devices weekly, best paired with netting since birds soon get used to scares.
  borer_stem_surgery           applies_to=['insect_boring']
      MEANS: A vine already wilting or pushing frass, where the grub is inside the stem and past the reach of any spray. Most worth attempting in July or early August, on a plant you would otherwise lose.
  codling_moth_pheromone_trap  applies_to=['insect_boring', 'insect_general']
      MEANS: Monitoring moth flight on a backyard apple tree so you can time the other steps well, not as a stand-alone control.
  exclusion_fencing            applies_to=['vertebrate']
      MEANS: Mammals that raid a ripening crop and cannot be sprayed or scared off, above all raccoons in sweet corn, with the fence energized before the crop is ready. Distinct from bird netting, which drapes mesh over the plants themselves against birds; this is a barrier around the bed, set at the height the animal travels.
      CAUTION: An electric fence is a real shock hazard to children and pets; use a fence energizer sold for the purpose, follow its instructions, and check local rules before installing one
  floating_row_cover           applies_to=['any']
      MEANS: Preventive exclusion from transplanting until the pest's egg-laying period passes, for cabbageworms, flea beetles and cabbage maggot. It is also the main defense against a disease with no cure once it arrives, such as bacterial wilt, where keeping the carrier insect off young plants is the lever that works. Remove for pollination if the crop needs it.
      CAUTION: Do not lay row cover over a bed where the same-family crop grew last year, or emerging soil pests can be trapped under it with the crop
  fruit_bagging                applies_to=['insect_boring', 'insect_general']
      MEANS: A spray-free way to protect a manageable number of apples on a backyard tree from codling moth and apple maggot, worth the labor when you do not have too many fruit.
  handpick                     applies_to=['insect_chewing', 'insect_general', 'mollusk']
      MEANS: Low to moderate numbers of anything big enough to spot and slow enough to catch, on a regular scouting routine: cabbageworms, cutworms, beetles, squash bugs, slugs and snails. Best in a small garden where you walk the rows anyway.
  kaolin_clay                  applies_to=['insect_boring', 'insect_chewing', 'insect_general']
      MEANS: An organic-acceptable repellent barrier for apple maggot, and partial help against plum curculio and codling moth, on backyard trees where you will reapply through the season.
      CAUTION: University trials show heavy use can harm beneficial species and lead to a flare-up of European red mites or San Jose scale
  prune_out_infection          applies_to=['bacterial', 'disease_general']
      MEANS: Cutting an infection out of a stem or branch by taking the cut well beyond the visible margin, back into clean tissue, and destroying what comes off. The core fire blight control on a backyard apple or pear. Distinct from garden sanitation, which is where simply picking off a spotted leaf or a rotted fruit, or pulling a plant that cannot be saved, belongs.
      CAUTION: Dipping shears in 10% bleach between cuts is wise, but placing the cut far enough below the infection matters more
  red_sphere_trap              applies_to=['insect_general']
      MEANS: Trapping out apple maggot flies on a backyard tree, using enough baited spheres per tree and hanging them by mid-June before egg-laying begins.
  slug_traps_barriers          applies_to=['mollusk']
      MEANS: Knocking down slug numbers around a strawberry bed with regular night handpicking, board or beer traps, and copper barriers, alongside removing their daytime hiding places.
  soil_solarization            applies_to=['bacterial', 'disease_general', 'fungal_soilborne', 'nematode']
      MEANS: A bed with a known soil-borne problem, treated in the off season before replanting. Most worthwhile where a nematode or root-rot history has already cost you a crop.
      CAUTION: Needs a genuinely hot, sunny stretch. In cool, windy or cloudy conditions the soil may never reach the temperatures that make it work.
  stem_collars                 applies_to=['insect_chewing', 'insect_general']
      MEANS: Protecting individual transplants from cutworms during establishment, most practical in small gardens.
  swd_exclusion_netting        applies_to=['insect_general']
      MEANS: Excluding spotted wing drosophila from a strawberry planting, with fine no-see-um-grade mesh put on before the first fruit colors and sealed at the edges so flies cannot enter.
  swd_monitoring_traps         applies_to=['insect_general']
      MEANS: Detecting when spotted wing drosophila arrives at a strawberry planting so the other steps are timed right; hang before fruit ripens and check weekly.
  water_spray                  applies_to=['insect_soft_bodied', 'mite']
      MEANS: Early aphid clusters on sturdy plants, as a first physical knock-back before considering any spray.
      CAUTION: Spray early in the day so foliage dries quickly, since prolonged leaf wetness can promote disease
  yellow_sticky_traps          applies_to=['insect_general', 'insect_soft_bodied']
      MEANS: Monitoring and reducing adult fungus gnats around trays and houseplants, and catching winged aphids and other small flying pests.

## biological
  augmentative_release         applies_to=['insect_chewing', 'insect_general', 'insect_soft_bodied', 'mite']
      MEANS: An active, located infestation you can release onto, once conservation is already in place, and realistically a large planting or a shared garden rather than a few rows. Distinct from beneficial predators, which is about CONSERVING the natural enemies already present and costs nothing; this one is bought, and its results are more conditional.
      CAUTION: Buy a release only for an infestation you have actually found; releasing ahead of the pest wastes it
  beneficial_nematodes         applies_to=['insect_general']
      MEANS: For soil-stage larvae such as fungus gnats in trays, drenched into moist media when the soil is warm.
  beneficial_predators         applies_to=['insect_general', 'insect_soft_bodied']
      MEANS: The default living control for aphids and other soft-bodied pests; lean on it before reaching for any spray.
      CAUTION: Avoid broad-spectrum insecticides, which kill the beneficials as well as the pests
  biofungicide                 applies_to=['fungal_foliar']
      MEANS: The first spots of a leaf fungus on a crop you would rather not put sulfur on, applied early and repeated. Distinct from sulfur, which is more effective and sits one rung further up; reach past this one when the mildew is already established.
      CAUTION: Live cultures have a shelf life and a storage temperature; an old or heat-stored bottle may do nothing at all
  bt                           applies_to=['insect_chewing']
      MEANS: The go-to biological control for caterpillars, from cabbageworms and loopers to hornworms, corn earworm and stalk borers. Spray on young caterpillars and reapply after rain or new growth. It acts on caterpillars as a group, so keep it off plants you are growing for butterflies.
      CAUTION: Bt kurstaki kills the caterpillars of moths and butterflies as a group, including desirable species such as swallowtails and monarchs; spray only plants with a pest problem, never butterfly host plants

## soft_chemical
  copper_fungicide             applies_to=['bacterial', 'disease_general', 'fungal_foliar']
      MEANS: A preventive, softer-chemical option for foliar diseases such as downy mildew and bacterial leaf spots when cultural steps are not holding; apply before or at the first sign of disease.
      CAUTION: Copper is highly to very highly toxic to fish and aquatic life; do not let spray or runoff reach ponds, streams, or storm drains
      CAUTION: Copper accumulates at the soil surface and persists, so limit repeat use over the years
      CAUTION: Follow the label rate; too much copper can injure foliage
      CAUTION: Which copper compound you buy changes the acute hazard, so read the active ingredient on the label rather than the brand name. UC IPM rates acute toxicity to people and other mammals Low for copper octanoate, the copper soap named on home-garden products, and for copper ammonium complex, Moderate for copper oxychloride sulfate, and High, the DANGER signal-word band, for copper hydroxide; none of the four carries a California Prop 65 or US EPA chronic listing.
  horticultural_oil            applies_to=['insect_general', 'insect_soft_bodied', 'mite']
      MEANS: A low-residue contact spray for spider mites and soft-bodied insects such as aphids once the softer steps are not holding, applied for thorough coverage in mild weather.
      CAUTION: Do not use oils on water-stressed plants or when temperatures exceed 90°F
      CAUTION: Do not apply sulfur within 2 weeks of an oil spray, since the combination can injure foliage
      CAUTION: UC IPM's honey bee precaution for horticultural oil: do not apply it, or let it drift, onto anything in flower including weeds, except between sunset and midnight where the label allows, and do not let it reach water bees can drink such as puddles.
  insecticidal_soap            applies_to=['insect_soft_bodied', 'mite']
      MEANS: Early, light aphid or other soft-bodied infestations you can coat directly, when you want minimal impact on beneficials.
      CAUTION: Can burn foliage on water-stressed plants or when the temperature is above 90°F
      CAUTION: Kills soft-bodied beneficials it directly wets, so spray only where pests are
      CAUTION: UC IPM rates acute toxicity to people and other mammals Moderate for potassium salts of fatty acids; wear chemical resistant gloves, long sleeves and goggles when mixing and spraying, since many consumer products do not print protective equipment on the label. Its chronic rating is no known risk, and its honey bee precaution is UC IPM's lowest band.
  iron_phosphate_slug_bait     applies_to=['mollusk']
      MEANS: A lower-risk bait to back up traps and sanitation against slugs in a strawberry bed, scattered on the soil rather than on the plants.
      CAUTION: Even a lower-risk bait is still a pesticide; scatter it on the soil, not on the plants, and follow the label
  neem_oil                     applies_to=['insect_general', 'insect_soft_bodied']
      MEANS: Light, early soft-bodied infestations, especially where you want a low-residue option that spares beneficials once it dries.
      CAUTION: UC IPM's honey bee precaution for neem oil: do not apply it, or let it drift, onto anything in flower including weeds, except between sunset and midnight where the label allows, and do not let it reach water bees can drink such as puddles.
      CAUTION: Do not apply in high heat above 90°F or to drought-stressed plants
  spinosad                     applies_to=['insect_chewing', 'insect_general']
      MEANS: A stronger organic rescue for caterpillar, thrips, or leafminer pressure the softer rungs have not held; time it to spare bees.
      CAUTION: Toxic to bees when sprayed and for about a day afterward; do not apply to flowering plants, and spray at dusk when bees are not foraging
  sulfur                       applies_to=['disease_general', 'fungal_foliar', 'mite']
      MEANS: Preventive control of powdery mildew and some mites on crops that tolerate it, applied when weather favors disease but temperatures are moderate.
      CAUTION: Do not apply when the temperature is near or above 90°F or to drought-stressed plants; sulfur burns foliage in heat
      CAUTION: Do not apply sulfur within 2 weeks of an oil spray
      CAUTION: Can harm released predatory (beneficial) mites unless they are sulfur-resistant, disrupting natural mite control

## conventional
  carbaryl                     applies_to=['insect_boring', 'insect_chewing', 'insect_general']
      MEANS: A rescue-only, last-resort option for beetle or caterpillar outbreaks the softer rungs have not held; not a first or routine choice.
      CAUTION: UC IPM puts carbaryl in its strictest honey bee band: do not apply it, or let it drift, onto anything in flower including weeds, and do not let it reach water bees can drink such as puddles.
      CAUTION: Rated Moderate to High for harm to natural enemies, and the residue is long lasting, so a spray can remove the predators that were holding a second pest down and let that pest flare weeks later.
      CAUTION: UC IPM rates acute toxicity to people and other mammals Low, the least severe of its four bands, so the short term risk is the mild half of this ingredient's picture.
      CAUTION: Listed on both the California Prop 65 list and the US EPA list, where an active ingredient appears only as a likely or confirmed carcinogen. On a food crop that listing is the reason to think twice, rather than the acute rating above.
      CAUTION: Rated Moderate for water quality risk to aquatic wildlife; keep spray and runoff away from ponds, streams, storm drains and puddles.
      CAUTION: Toxic to earthworms, which are important to healthy soil
      CAUTION: Many consumer products do not print protective equipment on the label; wear chemical resistant gloves, long sleeves and goggles regardless
      CAUTION: Observe the pre-harvest interval on the label before eating the crop, and always read and follow the label
  chlorothalonil               applies_to=['fungal_foliar']
      MEANS: A rescue-only last resort for a foliar disease the cultural and soft rungs have not held, on a crop whose own guidance names it. Preventive by nature, so it buys nothing applied late. Distinct from copper fungicide and sulfur, which sit below it and are the softer options to exhaust first; this is not a routine choice and a reader who declines it has not skipped a step they owed.
      CAUTION: Rated High for water quality risk to aquatic wildlife; keep spray and runoff away from ponds, streams, storm drains and puddles
      CAUTION: UC IPM rates acute toxicity to people and other mammals High, the DANGER signal-word band, and notes the rating follows the most sensitive route of entry rather than oral toxicity
      CAUTION: Listed on both the California Prop 65 list and the US EPA list, where an active ingredient appears only as a likely or confirmed carcinogen; weigh that before choosing it on a food crop
      CAUTION: UC IPM's honey bee precaution for chlorothalonil: do not apply it, or let it drift, onto anything in flower including weeds, except between sunset and midnight where the label allows, and do not let it reach water bees can drink such as puddles.
      CAUTION: Many consumer products do not print protective equipment on the label; wear chemical resistant gloves, long sleeves and goggles regardless
      CAUTION: Observe the pre-harvest interval on the label before eating the crop, and read and follow the label every time
  pyrethroid                   applies_to=['insect_boring', 'insect_chewing', 'insect_general', 'insect_soft_bodied']
      MEANS: A rescue-only, last-resort option for a heavy infestation that the softer rungs have not controlled; never a routine or first choice.
      CAUTION: UC IPM puts every common pyrethroid in its strictest honey bee band: do not apply one, or let it drift, onto anything in flower including weeds, and do not let it reach water bees can drink such as puddles.
      CAUTION: Rated High to Very High for water quality risk to aquatic wildlife depending on the ingredient; keep spray and runoff away from ponds, streams, storm drains and puddles.
      CAUTION: Rated Low to High for harm to natural enemies, most of the common ones at High, and the residue persists for weeks. Removing the predators is why a pyrethroid can leave aphids or mites worse off a month later than they were before the spray.
      CAUTION: Which pyrethroid you buy changes the health picture, so read the active ingredient on the label rather than the brand name. On the US EPA list, where an active ingredient appears only as a likely or confirmed carcinogen: permethrin, bifenthrin, cypermethrin, zeta-cypermethrin. Rated no known risk: cyfluthrin, beta-cyfluthrin, deltamethrin, esfenvalerate, lambda-cyhalothrin.
      CAUTION: UC IPM rates acute toxicity to people and other mammals Moderate for most pyrethroids and High for bifenthrin, cyfluthrin and beta-cyfluthrin.
      CAUTION: More toxic to cats than to dogs or people; keep pets off until the spray is dry
      CAUTION: Many consumer products do not print protective equipment on the label; wear chemical resistant gloves, long sleeves and goggles regardless
      CAUTION: Observe the pre-harvest interval on the label before eating the crop, and always read and follow the label
