## The leaves today

* **eggplant** `diseases.3.prevention_beginner`: Plant on raised beds or mounds so water drains away, avoid wet low spots, water at the soil, and keep fruit off soggy ground. Rotate away from peppers, tomatoes, and squash.
* **bell-pepper** `diseases.1.prevention_beginner`: Plant on raised beds or mounds so water drains away, avoid wet low spots, water at the soil and do not overwater, and rotate away from peppers for several years. Keeping the ground from staying soggy is the key.
* **banana-pepper** `diseases.1.prevention_beginner`: Plant on raised beds or mounds so water drains away, avoid wet low spots, water at the soil and do not overwater, and rotate away from peppers for several years. Keeping the ground from staying soggy is the key.
* **pumpkin** `soil_prep_beginner`: Pick a sunny spot with rich, well-drained soil and mix in plenty of compost before planting, since pumpkins are large, hungry plants. Plant them on low hills or mounds, which warm up and drain better. Give them lots of room: space hills at least 8 feet apart on all sides for full-size pumpkins, since the vines can spread 10 to 20 feet (small and bush types need less). If your soil is heavy or wet, build the mounds up higher so water drains away from the roots and fruit.
* **butternut-squash** `soil_prep_beginner`: Pick a sunny spot with rich, well-drained soil and mix in plenty of compost before planting, since butternut is a large, hungry plant. Plant it on low hills or mounds, which warm up and drain better. Give it lots of room: space plants about 2 to 3 feet apart in rows 5 to 6 feet apart for full-size butternut, since the vines can spread 8 to 12 feet (bush types like Butterbush need less). If your soil is heavy or wet, build the mounds up higher so water drains away from the roots and fruit.
* **acorn-squash** `soil_prep_beginner`: Pick a sunny spot with rich, well-drained soil and mix in plenty of compost before planting, since acorn squash is a hungry plant. Plant it on low hills or mounds, which warm up and drain better. Space it to the type: bush kinds like Table King can go about a foot and a half to two feet apart, while vining kinds need more room, about 2 to 3 feet apart in rows 5 to 6 feet apart. If your soil is heavy or wet, build the mounds up higher so water drains away from the roots and fruit.
* **spaghetti-squash** `soil_prep_beginner`: Pick a sunny spot with rich, well-drained soil and mix in plenty of compost before planting, since spaghetti squash is a large, hungry plant. Plant it on low hills or mounds, which warm up and drain better. Give it room: space plants about 2 to 3 feet apart in rows 5 to 6 feet apart, since the vines can spread 6 to 8 feet (bush types like Tivoli need less). If your soil is heavy or wet, build the mounds up higher so water drains away from the roots and fruit.
* **parsnip** `diseases.0.prevention_beginner`: Grow a canker-resistant variety, do not plant parsnips or their relatives in the same bed for two to three years, mound a little soil over the root shoulders during the season, keep carrot rust fly down, avoid soggy soil, and clear and bury old parsnip plants after harvest. (UMass)
* **parsnip** `failure_diagnostics.3.next_season_tip_beginner`: Grow a canker-resistant variety, do not plant parsnips or their relatives in the same bed for three years, mound a little soil over the root shoulders during the season, keep carrot rust fly down, and avoid soggy soil. Sprays do not fix root canker. (UMass)
* **parsnip** `growth_stages.2.user_action_beginner`: Keep the watering steady, about 1 inch a week, so the roots do not crack, and pull a little soil or mulch over the root tops as they show. They usually need no feeding. Wear gloves and long sleeves when handling the leaves on a sunny day, because parsnip sap can give some people a skin rash.
