## The entry today (bell-pepper)

* `symptoms_seasoned`: Dark, water-soaked lesions girdle the stem at or near the soil line and the plant wilts and collapses suddenly, sometimes with a white cottony growth; roots rot, and fruit can develop a firm, white-mold rot. It flares in warm, wet, poorly drained ground and after heavy rain or flooding, and is the most serious pepper disease in much of the country.
* `symptoms_beginner`: The plant wilts and collapses suddenly, often with a dark rotten patch on the stem near the soil, and the roots and even the fruit can rot. It strikes in warm, wet, soggy ground and moves fast. It is one of the worst pepper diseases.
* `cause_seasoned`: Phytophthora capsici, a water mold that thrives in saturated soil and spreads in splashing and running water. It survives in soil and debris for years and moves fast in warm, wet weather, especially on heavy or poorly drained ground and in low spots that stay wet.
* `cause_beginner`: A mold-like disease that lives in wet soil and spreads in splashing and running water, attacking peppers in warm, soggy conditions. It moves fast once it starts and lives in the soil for years.
* `organic_treatment_seasoned`: Remove and destroy affected plants and fruit promptly, and improve drainage immediately. There is no effective home-garden cure once plants collapse, so control is preventive and drainage-based.
* `organic_treatment_beginner`: Pull and destroy affected plants and fruit, and fix the drainage. There is no good home cure once it takes hold, so prevention is what matters.
* `prevention_seasoned`: Plant on raised, well-drained beds or hills, avoid low spots that stay wet, water at the soil rather than overhead, do not overwater, mulch to limit splash, rotate away from peppers and other susceptible crops for at least three to four years, and keep fruit up off saturated soil.
* `prevention_beginner`: Plant on raised beds or mounds so water drains away, avoid wet low spots, water at the soil and do not overwater, and rotate away from peppers for several years. Keeping the ground from staying soggy is the key.
* `control_ladder[improve_drainage]`: seasoned: Raised, well-drained beds deny this water mold the saturated soil it thrives in, and low spots that stay wet are where it starts; with no effective home-garden cure once plants collapse, drainage correction is the immediate response as well as the prevention. / beginner: Plant on raised beds or mounds so water drains away fast, and avoid low spots that stay wet after rain. This disease needs soggy soil, so drainage is the heart of the defense, and if it strikes, fix the drainage right away.
* `control_ladder[water_at_the_base]`: seasoned: Water at the soil and avoid excess; the pathogen spreads in splashing and running water and flares in saturated ground, so volume matters as much as placement. / beginner: Water at the soil rather than over the plants, and do not overwater. Warm, wet, soggy ground is exactly what lets this disease take off.
* `control_ladder[splash_barrier_mulch]`: seasoned: Mulch limits splash dispersal off infested soil, one of the routes by which the pathogen moves through a bed in wet weather. / beginner: Mulch under the plants to cut down on splashing, which is one of the ways the disease spreads.
* `control_ladder[crop_rotation]`: seasoned: Rotate away from peppers and other susceptible crops for at least three to four years; the pathogen persists in soil and debris for years, so a short gap buys little. / beginner: Move peppers to fresh ground and keep them, along with the other crops this disease attacks, out of a troubled bed for at least three to four years. It lives in the soil for years.
* `control_ladder[garden_sanitation]`: seasoned: Prompt removal and destruction of collapsed plants and affected fruit reduces the inoculum that splashing and running water carries onward through the bed. / beginner: Pull and destroy any plant that wilts and collapses, along with rotted fruit, as soon as you see it.
