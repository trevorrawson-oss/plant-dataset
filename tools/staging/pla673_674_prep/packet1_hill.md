## Notes (read-only)

* **The sources disagree on the sense.** Mound: Purdue (8-10 in high), UGA C1206 ("a few inches", "gentle mound"), CSU ("hills or mounds"), USU ("mounds"). Group, explicitly NOT a mound: UGA B577 ("a cluster of plants, not a mound of soil"). Group, made in a HOLE: NMSU ("make a hole in the soil, then drop four or five seeds"). NC State calls the group practice "hilling", the potato word.
* **Mound height:** stated only by Purdue (8-10 in) and C1206 ("a few inches"). **No page states a diameter**: the pumpkin/butternut `soil_prep_seasoned` "mounds about a foot across" remains on no hashed page.
* **"Thinned to the strongest one to three plants" (Iowa State per the handoff): UNDETERMINED.** On no Iowa State page reached (melons d84b3b2f, cucumbers cc412f7c, squash 85fb3c37 "thin to 2 to 3 vigorous, well-spaced plants per hill", all-about-pumpkins 106b8d20 the same; four ISU urls 404). The "one to three" wording is NMSU's (quote above).
* UGA B577, NCSU ch. 16, Purdue and CSU are cited on NO hill crop today; citing them is a promote decision.
